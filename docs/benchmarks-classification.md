## Disclosures

- **Section gold is Claude-annotated, for evaluation only.** 2194 sections of the 113 documents, at every heading depth (up to 30 per document, a third reserved for level 1), labelled blind against the section catalogue (v2); 30% are `none`, a heading with no domain meaning; 439 were double-annotated: Cohen's κ 0.914 at leaf and 0.931 at group, 34 disagreements adjudicated. The annotators were all Claude (the same underlying model), so κ measures consistency, not correctness.
- **Gold-split doc-type numbers are development numbers; the held-out run is the clean measure.** The type definitions and the section catalogue were refined after looking at gold results; catalogue v2 (2026-10-22) replaced the generic `general` tags with `none` and added a `government` group, and the section gold was labelled afresh against it.
- **Doc-type gold comes from the source**: the manifest's source-assigned type (`benchmarks/classification/doc_type_gold.json`), with 0 correction(s).
- **Only 8 of the 19 doc types are in the corpus** (`financial.annual_report`, `financial.fact_sheet`, `financial.prospectus`, `government.government_report`, `legal.contract`, `legal.court_opinion`, `research.research_paper`, `research.technical_standard`); the rules and the classifier are not validated on the other 11. Macro-F1 is over the gold types; section macro-F1 is over the gold leaves. The held-out corpus covers 18 types, 2 to 3 documents each.
- **Tokens are exact or estimated, as the Tokens column says.** Exact counts come from the API's `usage` (LLM backends). Keyword and embedding backends make no LLM call: their tokens estimate what the same views and options would cost an LLM. Section tagging tokens count in each document's tokens. The split column (options / content / instructions) is always an estimate, even next to exact totals: it shows how much of the prompt the option lists take.
- **Calls.** `per_item` asks one item per call; `batched` asks a whole stage per call with each option listed once (two steps: groups, then the leaves of each group); `batched_flat` lists every leaf once. Keyword and embedding backends always classify one item at a time: their `batched` rows differ from `per_item` only in the token estimate (what an LLM would be sent).
- **Over the cap.** Every call is budgeted to a 16,000-token target prompt: `full` cuts the document view head-first to fit it, and batched section calls are packed (at most 40 views, and an estimated 85% of the cap, per call), a view too large for any call being cut and sent alone. The over-cap table covers every call of both stages, for each run with a `full` level: the share of documents with an exact call over 16,000 tokens, the largest exact call (estimated runs have none), the share whose deciding document view was cut, and the share with any view (section or document) cut.
- **Every heading is tagged**, at any depth, up to 400 per document (level 1 first, then level 2 and so on, in reading order). A section past the cap takes its nearest tagged ancestor's tag, with no call (since 2026-10-22); one with no tagged domain ancestor is untagged and counts as a miss: the Sections column gives scored / excluded (reading mismatch) / untagged.
- **Section scores treat `none` as an abstention.** Precision is the share of domain tags given that were right (a domain tag on a gold `none` section is wrong); recall is the share of gold domain sections tagged right; macro-F1 averages the domain leaves in the gold. How often a gold `none` section was left `none` is reported apart.
- **1 document(s) have no titled sections** in the reading (profile `fast`), so they hold no section gold.
- **Token share** is a run's prompt tokens over those of the same backend, calls and flow's `full`/`full` run, summed over the same documents.
- **Failed documents** (the Errors column) count as wrong in accuracy and carry no tokens, so they are left out of the token means.
