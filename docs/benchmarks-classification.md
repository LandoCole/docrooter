# Classification benchmark: accuracy against tokens

How much accuracy each rung of the context ladder (`headings`, `terms`, `lead`, `full`) buys, and at what token cost, against sending the whole document (spec G5). The set is the 113 public documents of the gold split; the frozen held-out corpus is scored for doc type only, once, at the end. Generated by `python -m benchmarks.classification_bench report` from the results files; do not edit by hand.

**Results pending.** The grid has not been run yet.

## Disclosures

- **Section gold is Claude-annotated, for evaluation only.** 951 level-1 and level-2 sections of the 113 documents, labelled blind against the section catalogue; 190 were double-annotated: Cohen's κ 0.923 at leaf and 0.993 at group, 14 disagreements adjudicated. The annotators were all Claude (the same underlying model), so κ measures consistency, not correctness.
- **Doc-type rules were revised once after a reviewer checked them against the section gold. Gold-split doc-type numbers are therefore development numbers; the held-out run is the clean measure.** The revision was made from the doc-type definitions (an ordinary contract clause no longer makes a terms policy, and a contract no longer needs a titled parties or signatures section), not tuned to the gold labels.
- **Doc-type gold comes from the source**: the manifest's source-assigned type (`benchmarks/classification/doc_type_gold.json`), with 0 correction(s).
- **Only 8 of the 19 doc types are in the corpus** (`financial.annual_report`, `financial.fact_sheet`, `financial.prospectus`, `government.government_report`, `legal.contract`, `legal.court_opinion`, `research.research_paper`, `research.technical_standard`); the rules and the classifier are not validated on the other 11. Macro-F1 is over the gold types; section macro-F1 is over the gold leaves.
- **Tokens are exact or estimated, as the Tokens column says.** Exact counts come from the API's `usage` (LLM backends). Keyword and embedding backends make no LLM call: their tokens estimate what the same views and options would cost an LLM. Section tagging tokens count in each document's tokens. The split column (options / content / instructions) is always an estimate, even next to exact totals: it shows how much of the prompt the option lists take.
- **Calls.** `per_item` asks one item per call; `batched` asks a whole stage per call with each option listed once (two steps: groups, then the leaves of each group); `batched_flat` lists every leaf once. Keyword and embedding backends always classify one item at a time: their `batched` rows differ from `per_item` only in the token estimate (what an LLM would be sent).
- **Over the cap.** Every call is budgeted to a 16,000-token target prompt: `full` cuts the document view head-first to fit it, and batched section calls are packed (at most 40 views, and an estimated 85% of the cap, per call), a view too large for any call being cut and sent alone. The over-cap table covers every call of both stages, for each run with a `full` level: the share of documents with an exact call over 16,000 tokens, the largest exact call (estimated runs have none), the share whose deciding document view was cut, and the share with any view (section or document) cut.
- **At most 40 sections** (level 1 first, then level 2, in reading order) are tagged per document. A gold section past the cap is untagged and counts as a miss: the Sections column gives scored / excluded (reading mismatch) / untagged.
- **7 documents have no level-1/2 sections** in the reading (profile `fast`), so they hold no section gold; `sections_first` falls back to `doc_first` for them (flag `no_sections`).
- **doc_first section labels** come from the predicted doc type's own section taxonomy and are scored through the catalogue's `from` mapping, which reaches only 61 of the 112 catalogue leaves (from 67 per-type leaves). The 43 gold documents whose type maps to `section_types/generic` (7 leaves) are capped hardest, so doc_first's section F1 is structurally below sections_first's. A label with no catalogue leaf counts as no prediction (it costs recall, not precision).
- **Token share** is a run's prompt tokens over those of the same backend, calls and flow's `full`/`full` run, summed over the same documents.
- **Failed documents** (the Errors column) count as wrong in accuracy and carry no tokens, so they are left out of the token means.
